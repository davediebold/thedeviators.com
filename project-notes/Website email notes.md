# Website notes — email of 27 August 2026

Source: https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#all/1a043d2d93ba7ab5

Been chatting with someone who knows these things better than I do, and
here's some website suggestions that came up ---

   1.

   *Home — make the first screen do almost everything.* Use that jumping
   Grand Social image full-width with the distressed logo over/above it.
   Underneath, one plain-English line: *Dublin three-piece. Original
   high-energy punk, pop and rock ’n’ roll.* Then two obvious buttons: *Listen
   to Creatures* and *Watch live*. A promoter should understand the band in
   ten seconds without navigating anywhere.
   2.

   *Put the music on the site rather than sending people elsewhere to find
   it.* Embed *Creatures* directly from SoundCloud. When there are two or
   three finished tracks we’re happy with, add those. When *More Volume,
   Less Reverb* is released, that section becomes the album page: artwork,
   track listing, Stano production credit and Spotify/Bandcamp/Apple links.
   The album title is strong enough to be prominent visually.
   3.

   *Have a proper Live page.* At the top: forthcoming dates. *Last Days of
   Dalymount* should be there now, with ticket link, date and venue.
   Beneath that, a short *Selected past shows* section rather than an
   exhaustive gig diary: Rocky De Valera & the Gravediggers reunion — Grand
   Social; The Prongs — Annesley House; Grand Social debut, etc. As the list
   grows, the old gigs become useful evidence that we're an active band.
   4.

   *Replace a generic About page with a good short band story.* Start with
   about 150 words explaining The Deviators: three Dublin musicians with
   histories stretching back through the city's punk scene, now making new
   original material together. Then have Bitzy, Bren and Andy underneath with
   compact bios. Their histories are one of the band's genuine assets, so they
   should be easy to discover without making the band look backwards-facing.
   5.

   *Give the photographs a proper Media page.* Lead with the jumping Grand
   Social photograph. Then maybe 8–12 genuinely strong live shots, not 40
   near-duplicates. Have the black-and-white trio photograph available too,
   and eventually arrange one deliberately shot press photograph of all three.
   Add photographer credits where we have them. Ideally provide a
little *Download
   hi-res press photos* button for journalists/promoters.
   6.

   *Put those press quotes somewhere visible.* Not an entire “Reviews” page
   yet — that would look thin. The Goo quote would look terrific between
   sections on the homepage:

   *“The songs were riotous and the crowd ate up every second of it.”*
   *— The Goo*

   Then Colm O’Hare’s “Terrific gig…” quote nearer the live section. As
   reviews accumulate, they can grow into a proper Press page.
   7.

   *Give Bitzy’s book a small sidebar/detail on his bio.* *Past the Point
   of Rescue* being into multiple printings adds colour and tells people
   that there’s more cultural history around the band than three CVs. Link to
   somewhere people can buy it.
   8.

   *Add an EPK/Press page when we have final approved draft.* This is
   important. Something like *Press / EPK* in the menu, containing:
   Download EPK PDF; short bio; long bio; press quotes; music; live video;
   hi-res photos; logo; management contact.
   9.

   *Make contact details impossible to miss.* Footer on every page: *Bookings
   / Management: Dave Diebold · dave.diebold@gmail.com
   <dave.diebold@gmail.com> · 087 997 3953*. Plus social icons. No
   anonymous contact form as the only means of reaching the band; industry
   people like knowing who they're contacting.
   10.

   *Keep the design slightly rough.* The logo has attitude. The photography
   has attitude. Let those carry it: lots of black, white, grey, big
   photographs, straightforward typography and occasional bits of the
   distressed logo treatment. It should look like somebody in the band made a
   very good punk website.

There’s also a subtle positioning change that could be made throughout. *Don't
describe Deviators primarily as a heritage punk band.* The history should
sit underneath the current band. The order is: *here's what The Deviators
sound like now → here's what they're doing now → incidentally, look at
where these three came from.* That prevents all those impressive
previous-band credits from accidentally making *More Volume, Less Reverb*
look like a nostalgia exercise.
I think we could make the website materially better without re-inventing
very much at all. Almost all the good content already exists; it just needs
putting in the right order.
I can get my tech lad Zach to help us out (no fee) if we agree on any of
these suggestions.
